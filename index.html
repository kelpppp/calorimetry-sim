Welcome to the Calorimetry Simulation

What is calorimetry?

Calorimetry is an experimental technique used to measure heat transfer during a chemical or physical process. By measuring the final equilibrium temperature of a system, the specific heat capacity of an unknown substance can be determined.

Calorimetry relies on thermal equilibrium and the Law of Conservation of Energy. When two objects at different temperatures come into thermal contact, heat flows from the hotter object to the cooler one until they reach the same temperature. In an ideal insulated calorimeter, no energy is exchanged with the surroundings.

Simulation overview

In this simulation, a metal initially heated to 100 °C is placed in thermal contact with 0.20 kg of water at 20 °C inside an insulated calorimeter.

You will conduct two separate investigations, each consisting of five trial runs. In each investigation, you will vary either the mass or the type of metal while keeping the other input constant.

After completing the trials, you will receive a data table, a graph of your results, and a step-by-step explanation of how the metal's specific heat capacity can be inferred from the simulated data.

Note: This simulation makes simplifying assumptions and contains limitations. For more information, click “About the model” at the top of the page.
